<h1 align="center">The Civilization Engine</h1>

<p align="center"><strong>A living-world simulator where people build their own communities, economies and institutions.</strong></p>

<p align="center">People make a living, raise families, trade, learn, build and govern. Their choices shape the societies that emerge.</p>

The Civilization Engine (TCE) is a long-running simulation project. It models people, households and settlements from the ground up: how they find food, learn skills, build, trade, make rules and respond to events. The engine supplies the materials and social mechanisms; it does not script a civilization's story or destination.

The current playable world is an early farming society. The project roadmap reaches industrial and modern civilization, but those systems are still being built. The Unreal client is also future work.

| M6b · Fire, masonry and floods | Done (its open items close this entry): buildings burn and spread fire; people notice, raise alarms, carry water, strip threatened roofs and salvage goods; those latter choices remain available without a known water route. The dashboard has a Fires row for household escapes and a preliminary ungraded Floods and storms row; its exposure accounting remains partial. Masonry, brick and tile, flood depths and wet-floor damage, evacuation, storm uplift and flood/storm kernel commands are implemented. River overrides hold their requested days and route added flow downstream; forced storms change today's wind. Hazard origins and one uplift evaluation per covering per storm are saved. Flood exposure and floor loss advance daily; household supplies return to the chosen flood refuge. The chronicle sums household fire escapes monthly. An equipped crew can pull down an empty threatened building with ownership or leave on site; partial work saves. People tell dated accounts of buildings found burning through ordinary contact, without asserting a cause. Fire pages add dated notices and response work with physical reasons. Task awareness follows personal records and connected physical fire scenes. The Help at a fire lens separates one current availability probe from thirty days of recorded work. The 1–100/K household-year ignition sweep is measured and published; the Fires row separates observer-caused losses. Crew coordination and response calibration remain partial. BG begins with saved sub-grid bank crests, explicit borrow-earth accounting and conservative river-flood barriers, shown as lines in the observer. Ground beneath banks and borrow pits enters flood storage, including after a breach. Actual overtopping lowers bank crests and retains washed earth as sediment stock. Households remember what floods cost them; a gathering can pass a flood bank where it would have kept that water out, and people choose to dig, carry and place its earth in equal shares (one-segment banks on free ground only). Banks settle; water held against one side seeps through and can break them, and a broken bank is told in the chronicle. A gathering can pass a law to keep a bank it built, which asks the households to mend it after floods and each year; nobody mends a bank no law keeps. A household whose crop went short of water can dig an irrigation ditch to its field from the river and open it in dry spells; its water is debited from the river below and every loss is accounted for, and the map draws it. Households remember fires they lost a building to or saw; once someone knows how to fire tile, a gathering can pass a rule that new roofs near other buildings must not burn, which builders keep with tile or a plot apart, or break. Households also hear of buildings that give way; a gathering can ask that new walls be of stone or brick, or that new buildings stand apart. The M6b demo sets a fire in a village that had none of its own: it takes three huts, and the gathering passes walls of stone and a gap between buildings within two weeks, which bind nothing in the year after because the huts are mended rather than rebuilt. Left open: trapped rain, shared ditches, multi-segment banks, crossing scour and flood gates. |
| M6c · War and conquest | Implemented: households take from homes of other villages they have seen; bands are called, joined, mustered and walked to a household, where a two-sided encounter by the minute decides who gives way and what is carried off; a gathering may send a band by law, raise a palisade with gates kept shut, hear raids its own people made, seek terms with a word not to raid, take a village its band holds into one polity, and lose it again to a revolt there. | M7 · Politics deepens | Implemented (M7a, councils and elections; M7b, courts and precedent; M7c, trade laws and organizations; M7d, credit and money): offices may be held for a term and filled by lot from those who stood for them; a village may amend its custom to a council of 5 or 9 seats filled by election or by lot, whose election is called, told, stood for, polled at the hearth and counted by the block vote, every failure recorded; the council alone is the body then, and the label reads it as a republic. Those who stand say where they stand and campaign at the hearth; voters weigh distance, record and their faction's slate; a polity of several settlements may share its seats among them; factions that would lose apart may agree one slate. The M7a demo's village nudged one prior and took a council of 5 by 65 to 0, eight standing for its seats ([ADR-0024](decisions/0024-seats-offices-and-elections.md)). A law may name a forum to hear cases (one hearer, a panel by lot or the elders), whose findings are remembered and told and lean later sittings as its authority says; a limit adopted through the custom's amendment route forbids laws that keep people from daylight work or levy too much, and a forum given review may strike a breaching law down when someone it burdens challenges it; a ruling is known person by person, and what a forum finds is held against the forum ([ADR-0025](decisions/0025-forums-precedent-and-review.md)). M7c ([ADR-0026](decisions/0026-organizations-privileges-and-trade-policy.md)): a polity may lay a duty on tools its people buy at another settlement's door, paid to its store or kept back, or an embargo on a settlement's doors, kept or broken person by person. Those who sell the goods a duty would serve may organize, by rules of who is admitted and who decides; those turned away hold it against the organization, and a polity may recognize one, sparing its members its duties on those goods. A recognized organization may be granted a licence or a staple: others keep it or break it at their doors, its members bring breaches as cases, and a workshop that keeps it and sells nothing is given up as barred. A store's keeper may draw from it for their own household and, when it runs low, answer those they favour; a duty's collector may ask a buyer for a payment to assess nothing; a sponsor whose law would fail may give food to households it would bring round, who owe them for it at the gathering. A custom may be amended to close the gathering to members of recognized or privileged organizations, and the government panel says in sentences what an organization has come to hold. The M7c demo's villages passed an embargo and a duty on tools once a neighbour's door was used, but nobody organized: at this scale trade is too small to fight over. M7d ([ADR-0027](decisions/0027-claims-and-coin.md)): a household short of food before its harvest may borrow grain from a neighbour with food to spare, who weighs the loan by what it believes of the borrower and asks an increment; the loan is repaid after the harvest or, unpaid, defaulted and remembered. A lender that would refuse may lend against the use of a field or with a guarantor; at the due day a debtor pays, asks for time or refuses, and a failed loan's pledge is taken. A workshop short of a wage owes it and fails only if it is still unpaid when due. A village may pass a law of *debts heard*: a creditor then brings a loan left unpaid before the gathering, whose finding gives the debtor time or imposes what is owed. A village may also lend from its common store, at its law's terms, to households short before their harvest. Custody waits: no village household ever held more than its roof kept. Copper is content: ore on uplands, smelting found by those who fire kilns (or taught by the observer), and a cast copper axe that does an axe's work and lasts three times as long, made when it costs a household fewer hours for each hour of use. Copper is paid by weight, worth to its holder what it last bought, less beyond a few weeks' spending. Coin can be held issue by issue in purses, and a village may name a mint's keeper, to whom households bring copper for coin less a fee, when coin is worth more to them than its metal, its store may pay coin for the hours households give to its common works, and its levy may be paid in coin, which makes a piece worth the grain it pays for. Those who know smelting value coin by its copper and pay with the lightest; a village may strike lighter coin; another village's coin is taken by weight at a discount; the chronicle records a mint's first coin and the wealth panel tells each village's coin. The M7d demo's villages lent and borrowed grain within their first year without a nudge (84 loans over two seeds, 54 from common stores), but in four years nobody smelted copper: the village taught to smelt still held about 19 stone axes and never needed a new one, so no mint or coin arose. |

## Technical highlights

- **Simulation kernel:** Rust workspace with separate crates for world generation, land, people, content, simulation, hosting and a versioned C interface.
- **Emergent systems:** event-scheduled lives and household economies; authored building grammars; institutions formed through proposals and collective decisions; weather, water, illness, fire, conflict and transport.
- **Many-settlement scale:** each person remains individually represented. Settlements away from the observer's focus can resolve household work once per day, while the selected settlement continues in detail. Time skips use the same household machinery.
- **Industrial transition underway:** water-powered work is represented by saved installations whose daily output follows river discharge; powered recipes and water mills can be built when households expect them to repay their cost.
- **Persistence and interfaces:** checksummed, versioned snapshots; generated FlatBuffers messages; a localhost WebSocket observer that displays kernel state and forwards commands. Current boundary versions: wire 1.96, save schema 174 and content API 160.

## Run it

Install the pinned Rust toolchain and Node.js 22, then launch from the repository root:

```sh
./tools/run.sh          # macOS and Linux
```

On Windows, run `tools\run.ps1` in PowerShell. The observer opens at <http://127.0.0.1:7420/>. See [AGENTS.md](AGENTS.md) for development commands and change requirements.
