*Shimbashi, Tokyo, 1946. The archive records that stalls continued operating after the market’s official closure—a useful reminder that an illegal market need not be physically hidden.* [昭和館](https://search.showakan.go.jp/search/photo/detail.php?id=90255177)

# Informal and illegal economies: a design report for TCE

## Core recommendation

**Build one economy operating under overlapping legal, customary and coercive rules—not a separate “crime economy.”** The same household, merchant, warehouse or official should be able to participate in lawful trade, unregistered work, tax evasion and prohibited transactions.

The evidence supports two related but distinct processes:

**Illicit markets emerge when demand and feasible supply survive restrictions, and bypassing those restrictions offers an advantage. Organized crime emerges when durable cooperation, coercion or private governance becomes profitable.** Neither process guarantees the other: Reuter’s research finds illegal markets populated by small, unstable enterprises rather than inevitable mafia monopolies; Gambetta explains why other settings sustain specialized protection organizations. [MIT Press](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262181075/disorganized-crime/)

For TCE, the most important interacting variables are the **policy-created price gap, household need, access to trusted partners, appropriable commercial income, credible public protection and opportunities for collective coercion**. These should generate participation and organization endogenously.

## 1. Mechanisms: implementable causal rules

### 1.1 Separate informality, illegality and predation

“Informal employment,” “unreported production” and “organized crime” measure different things. The ILO’s employment estimates and macroeconomic shadow-economy estimates are not interchangeable, and neither directly measures mafia activity. [International Labour Organization](https://www.ilo.org/publications/women-and-men-informal-economy-statistical-picture-third-edition)

Use independent classifications rather than one legal/illegal switch:

| Dimension | TCE representation | Example |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Recognition | Registered, customary, unregistered, registration not applicable | A household workshop recognized by village custom but absent from a tax register |
| Compliance | Declared quantity, paid duty, license, permitted price | A licensed merchant concealing part of a shipment |
| Commodity/activity legality | Rules for production, possession, transport and sale | Production permitted, export prohibited |
| Employment status | Recognized contract and applicable protections | Informal workers inside an otherwise formal enterprise |
| Coercion | Voluntary exchange, disputed obligation, threat, seizure | Protection purchased against outsiders versus payment demanded by the threatening group |

**Implementation rule:** determine legality from `(jurisdiction, date, actor, activity, commodity, quantity, authorization)`. Changing a law should reclassify relevant activities without changing the physical goods.

For accounting, distinguish production from redistribution. In TCE’s welfare ledger, stealing grain transfers ownership; destroying it reduces stocks; transporting or processing it uses labor and creates services. Do not count the entire value of stolen goods or extortion payments as newly produced output.

### 1.2 Restrictions create opportunities, not automatic criminal participation

For each feasible activity, an agent compares its expected return with legal work, legal trade, household production and inactivity. This follows the expected-benefit/expected-sanction approach associated with Becker, but TCE should add imperfect information, social relationships and heterogeneous motivations. [RCNi Company Limited](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/abs/10.1086/259394)

A useful **proposed transaction model** is:

\[
E[\pi\_I]=(1-p\_s)P\_Iq-Cq-K-B-X-p\_jF
\]

Here, \(q\) is shipment quantity; \(P\_I\) is the realized sale price conditional on success; \(C\) is acquisition cost per unit; \(K\) is transport and handling cost; \(B\) and \(X\) are expected bribe and protection payments; \(p\_s\) is seizure probability before sale; and \(p\_jF\) is expected additional sanction cost.

This deliberately separates seizure from punishment. Acquisition cost is already paid, so do not subtract the seized inventory’s purchase value a second time. Imprisonment and injury should also affect the agent’s future availability and welfare, not merely remove money.

Participation additionally requires working capital, access to goods, a buyer and knowledge of an opportunity. A poor household can urgently need income yet lack the resources or contacts to enter a profitable trade.

**Tariff rule:** increasing the duty raises the advantage of noncompliance only to the extent that it increases the difference between legal and illicit net returns. The China–Hong Kong evidence finds a strong relationship between import taxes and reporting discrepancies, including shifts toward lower-taxed product classifications. It does not imply that every tariff increase produces an equally large increase in physical smuggling. [RCNi Company Limited](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/full/10.1086/381476)

**Prohibition rule:** remove authorized supply, not consumer preferences. Prices, inconvenience, sanctions and moral disapproval can reduce demand substantially, while some demand persists and alternative supply develops. The American Prohibition estimates below illustrate both effects. [National Bureau of Economic Research](https://www.nber.org/papers/w3675)

### 1.3 Binding price ceilings create allocation problems

A ceiling matters when the quantity people seek at the permitted price exceeds what authorized sellers actually supply:

\[
\text{unmet demand}=\max\left(0,D(P\_{\max})-Q\_{\text{authorized}}\right).
\]

Represent the resulting allocation explicitly: queues, ration entitlements, personal connections, favoritism, substitutions and unsuccessful shopping trips.

For buyers, the relevant comparison is not simply “cheap legal goods versus expensive illegal goods”:

\[
\text{full legal cost}
=
\text{posted price}
+
\text{time cost}
+
\text{expected cost of stockouts}.
\]

**Implementation rules:**

* Households first use affordable, accessible entitlements; unmet needs create demand for alternatives.
* Producers compare authorized sales, household retention, legal alternatives and unauthorized sales.
* Officials or intermediaries can divert inventory only when they actually control it.
* Repeated shortages affect future production and entry, subject to harvest and investment delays.
* Resale requires a real transfer of rationed goods; it must not duplicate them.

Postwar Japan demonstrates the importance of distribution failure: black markets expanded as rationing and distribution broke down, sometimes with official tolerance or covert collaboration. This was not simply a population becoming more criminal. [Sage Journals](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/00961442221078912)

A ceiling need not cause a large black market when supply is adequate, rationing works, subsidies sustain production or the ceiling is nonbinding. Likewise, a black-market price above the ceiling is not necessarily above the price that would prevail without controls.

### 1.4 Trust determines who can transact

When participants cannot safely use ordinary courts, the value of reputation, repeated dealings and intermediaries rises. But relationships also matter independently of illegality: the key change is the availability and reliability of outside enforcement.

TCE should reuse household, friendship, workplace and previous-trade relationships. Successful exchanges improve beliefs about reliability; defaults reduce them; introductions transmit imperfect reputational information.

A cheap proposed rule is:

\[
\text{cooperate when immediate cheating gain}
<
\text{expected lost future business}
+
\text{expected sanctions}.
\]

This allows trust-based trade without requiring a protection racket. It also creates exclusion: an outsider may face worse prices or be unable to transact despite having money. Multiplex-network models of organized-crime recruitment provide a concrete precedent for using overlapping social relationships rather than assigning people a fixed criminal identity. [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10940-020-09489-z)

### 1.5 Organized crime requires a reason to organize

Groups should form when collective activity yields more than independent activity after coordination, betrayal and enforcement costs.

Possible advantages include shared capital, dispute resolution, access to customers, political influence and credible protection. Countervailing costs include visibility, internal theft, succession disputes and expensive attempts to suppress competitors. Reuter’s evidence is particularly important here: **criminal organization does not imply successful monopoly**. [MIT Press](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262181075/disorganized-crime/)

For TCE, allow several outcomes: independent traders, temporary partnerships, durable merchant networks, neighborhood gangs and territorial protection organizations. Do not automatically promote every successful smuggler into a mafia boss.

Recruitment should consider earnings, legal alternatives, relationships, status, coercion and expected advancement. Levitt and Venkatesh’s gang accounts show highly unequal compensation and substantial danger; their published analysis concludes that immediate economic rewards alone do not adequately explain participation. [OUP Academic](https://academic.oup.com/qje/article-abstract/115/3/755/1828153)

### 1.6 Protection and extortion must be separable

Gambetta’s central contribution is to treat mafia protection as a business: it can enforce agreements, deter theft and regulate competition where trusted public guarantees are inadequate. Protection can provide something customers value while remaining coercive and socially damaging. [Google Books](https://books.google.com/books/about/The_Sicilian_Mafia.html?id=y3bv3tqWftYC)

Represent three distinct effects:

| Effect | What the organization does | Simulation consequence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Actual protection | Reduces theft or enforces agreements against outsiders | Clients may transact or invest more |
| Extortion | Threatens clients who refuse payment | Payment can occur without any genuine service |
| Exclusionary control | Restricts entry or customer access | Higher incumbent margins, fewer opportunities for outsiders |

A proposed client decision is to compare the fee with expected losses under each alternative: public protection, community protection, another organization, self-defense, relocation or refusal.

Keep the protector’s own retaliation separate from external threats. Otherwise the model will incorrectly count threatening someone and then refraining from violence as an unqualified welfare improvement.

### 1.7 Criminal governance can be quiet, bureaucratic and persistent

Do not equate criminal power with constant violence. Evidence from Brazil’s PCC describes credit-based trade, extensive records, collective benefits and predominantly nonviolent internal sanctions. This shows that criminal governance can operate through rules and reputation, not only personal intimidation. [Cambridge University Press](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/american-political-science-review/article/abs/legitimacy-in-criminal-governance-managing-a-drug-empire-from-behind-bars/15AA260804337B58B4A75EAF469457F6)

For TCE, violence should depend on contested claims, retaliation decisions, succession and the credibility of institutions. A powerful organization can collect payments with little immediate fighting; a weakened one can become more violent as competitors challenge it.

Organizations should also retain relationships, capital and political influence after their original revenue source disappears. Legalization may destroy one business opportunity without instantly dissolving the organization.

### 1.8 Enforcement has multiple, sometimes opposing effects

Replace a single “law enforcement strength” statistic with a chain:

**observation → investigation → identification → apprehension → adjudication → sanction → replacement or deterrence.**

A proposed exposure model is:

\[
p\_{\text{detected}}=1-\exp(-\lambda\,e\,\Delta t),
\]

where \(\lambda\) reflects local investigative capacity and \(e\) is exposure. Apply it over elapsed simulation time, not once per rendering frame.

| Intervention | Direct mechanism | Possible offset or failure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Inspections and investigations | Raise expected interruption or punishment | Displacement to other markets; higher prices |
| Auditing officials | Makes corruption more costly | Weak follow-through or capture elsewhere |
| Reliable courts and protection | Improves lawful alternatives and reporting | Benefits may exclude politically weak residents |
| Leadership arrests | Disrupt coordination and remove experienced actors | Succession contests and rival entry |
| Collective refusal and witness support | Reduces isolation of victims | Retaliation when protection is not credible |
| Legalization, lower duties or improved provisioning | Reduces the illicit advantage | Organizations diversify; fiscal or distributional trade-offs remain |

The evidence rejects both “enforcement always works” and “enforcement always backfires.” Indonesia’s randomized auditing experiment reduced missing public expenditures. Mexican studies find that some crackdowns displaced trafficking and intensified violent competition. These are different interventions acting through different mechanisms. [National Bureau of Economic Research](https://www.nber.org/papers/w11753)

## 2. Parameters and quantitative calibration

### 2.1 Informal activity relative to formal activity

The following are **employment**, not output or criminal-market, measures. They come from the ILO’s **2018 statistical edition**, rather than a claim about the latest year. “High confidence” means strong standardized evidence within that definition—not a timeless parameter for all societies. [International Labour Organization](https://www.ilo.org/publications/women-and-men-informal-economy-statistical-picture-third-edition)

| Geography | Informal employment / total employment | Implied informal : formal employment ratio\* | Confidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| World | 61.2% | 1.58 : 1 | High |
| Africa | 85.8% | 6.04 : 1 | High |
| Arab States | 68.6% | 2.18 : 1 | High |
| Asia and Pacific | 68.2% | 2.14 : 1 | High |
| Americas | 40.0% | 0.67 : 1 | High |
| Europe and Central Asia | 25.1% | 0.34 : 1 | High |

*Ratios are calculated as \(s/(1-s)\), treating the employment classification as a partition. They do not describe the ratio of illegal to legal production.*

For output, Medina and Schneider’s 2018 IMF working paper estimates an average shadow economy of **31.9% of official GDP** across 158 countries over 1991–2015. Selected country-period means range from **7.2% in Switzerland** to **62.3% in Bolivia**; Georgia is estimated at **64.9%**. These are model-dependent estimates, not direct censuses. [IMF](https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/wp/2018/wp1817.pdf)

Three cautions are essential. The study primarily targets concealed productive activity, not a clean criminal-sector total. Its macro indicators cannot perfectly separate categories. And official national accounts already make adjustments for some non-observed activity, so “official GDP plus estimated shadow GDP” is not automatically an exhaustive total. **Confidence: medium-to-low for precise levels; unsuitable as a direct mafia-share target.** [IMF](https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/wp/2018/wp1817.pdf)

### 2.2 Historical and behavioral anchors

| Quantity | Observed value or range | Definition and scope | TCE use and confidence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| British tea tax reform | **119% → 12.5%**, 1784 | Tax-rate change reported in Antman’s historical reconstruction | A large policy-wedge experiment. **High** for the reported rates; do not infer a universal smuggling elasticity. [IZA Docs](https://docs.iza.org/dp15016.pdf) |
| Import-tax sensitivity | **+1 percentage point tax associated with about +3% evasion**, as defined by the study | Fisman–Wei, China–Hong Kong product-level reporting gaps, principally 1998 data | Validate the direction and scale of reporting responses in a comparable scenario. **Medium**; not a three-percentage-point increase in the smuggled share. [RCNi Company Limited](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/full/10.1086/381476) |
| Initial US Prohibition consumption | About **30% of pre-Prohibition consumption** | Miron–Zwiebel reconstruction using indirect indicators | A severe initial contraction is possible despite continuing demand. **Medium-to-low** for the exact level. [National Bureau of Economic Research](https://www.nber.org/papers/w3675) |
| Later US Prohibition consumption | About **60–70% of pre-Prohibition consumption** | Same reconstruction | Adaptive recovery without full restoration. **Medium-to-low**; residual consumption is not synonymous with illegal sales. [National Bureau of Economic Research](https://www.nber.org/papers/w3675) |
| Postwar household provisioning | **5% rationed, 52% black-market, 43% barter** | Shares of food expenditure reported for **110 Osaka households**, November 1945, in a January 1946 translated press report | Stress-test mixed provisioning and asset depletion. **Low-to-medium**: narrow contemporary sample, not national shares or calorie shares. [Dartmouth College Library](https://collections.dartmouth.edu/teitexts/Press_Translations_Japanese/diplomatic/economic-0931-diplomatic.html) |
| Gang mortality | **0.07 violent deaths per member-year** | One gang’s four-year financial study | Extreme-risk scenario, not an ordinary criminal mortality parameter. **Medium within the case; very low transferability.** [National Bureau of Economic Research](https://www.nber.org/papers/w6592) |
| Audit intervention | Audit probability **4% → 100%**; missing expenditure falls about **8 percentage points** | Olken’s randomized study of more than 600 Indonesian village road projects | Evidence that credible monitoring can reduce diversion. **High for that intervention**, not a smuggling coefficient. [National Bureau of Economic Research](https://www.nber.org/papers/w11753) |

These numbers should calibrate **specific historical experiments**, not all TCE worlds simultaneously.

The Osaka observation is especially useful because it shows several procurement channels operating within ordinary household budgets. High black-market expenditure can reflect both reliance on unauthorized supply and unusually high prices; it does not establish that the same proportion of food volume came from that channel. [Dartmouth College Library](https://collections.dartmouth.edu/teitexts/Press_Translations_Japanese/diplomatic/economic-0931-diplomatic.html)

### 2.3 Proposed simulation priors—not empirical estimates

The literature does not provide portable values for the seizure probability of an agrarian caravan, the memory of a fictional merchant or the operating cost of a newly formed protection group. Those require explicit design assumptions and sensitivity testing.

| Parameter | Suggested initial exploration | Units | Status/source |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Ad valorem duty | 0–150% | Percentage of taxable consignment value | **Design range**, including historically large wedges |
| Ceiling severity | 0.3–1.2 | Ceiling divided by a no-control reference price | **Design range**; reference price is a diagnostic, not agent knowledge |
| Interruption probability | 0–40% | Per completed commercial trip | **Design range**; derive from exposure and capacity |
| Adjudication probability after identification | 5–90% | Conditional probability per case | **Design range** for institutional contrasts |
| Belief-memory half-life | 30–365 | Simulation days | **Design range** |
| Business-strategy reconsideration | 7–30 | Simulation days | **Engineering/behavioral prior** |
| Organization setup cost | 5–50 | Local median monthly earnings | **Design prior**; ongoing costs remain explicit |
| Additional active trade contacts | 8–32 | Contacts per economically active agent | **Engineering prior**, not a cap on family relationships |

Avoid a universal “protection fee = 10% of income” rule. Let demands respond to accessible cash flow, alternatives, coercive leverage and the organization’s time horizon. Likewise, **let black-market prices emerge from inventory and bargaining; do not impose a universal markup**.

## 3. Variation across eras and world regions

### 3.1 Eras should describe institutional configurations, not unlock crime types

| Setting | Relevant mechanisms | TCE treatment |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Foragers** | Customary obligations, sharing, reputation, exclusion and collective sanctions | Model norm violation and contested possession. Do not call all unregistered activity “informal,” or create customs smuggling without customs rules. Wiessner’s analysis of 308 Ju/’hoansi conversations supplies evidence for decentralized norm enforcement. [Arizona State University](https://asu.elsevierpure.com/en/publications/norm-enforcement-among-the-juhoansi-bushmen-a-case-of-strong-reci/) |
| **Early farming** | Stored output, fixed assets, tribute obligations and control over access create possible targets for concealment and appropriation | Allow these opportunities when the relevant institutions emerge. Quantitative “black-market shares” are poorly supported. Appropriability is a useful hypothesis, not a settled universal explanation of hierarchy; the cereal-based argument has attracted methodological criticism. [RCNi Company Limited](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/abs/10.1086/718372) |
| **Pre-industrial commercial states** | Customs boundaries, monopolies and trade bans interact with merchant networks and uneven enforcement | Make legal status jurisdiction-specific. Britain’s tea trade and Ming maritime restrictions offer different policy experiments, not one European developmental path. [OUP Academic](https://academic.oup.com/ahr/article-abstract/74/1/44/145763) |
| **Industrial and wartime societies** | Large urban markets, prohibition, rationing and disrupted provisioning | Permit widespread household participation, large throughput and visible unauthorized markets. US Prohibition and postwar Japan illustrate different demand and supply shocks. [National Bureau of Economic Research](https://www.nber.org/papers/w3675) |
| **Modern societies** | Extensive regulation can coexist with high informality; organizations may govern through credit, records and prison networks | Greater administrative sophistication need not eliminate illicit activity. Vary institutional coverage and accountability rather than applying a universal modernization decline. [World Bank](https://www.worldbank.org/en/research/brief/informal-economy-database) |

### 3.2 Regional cases and what they contribute

**Britain: tariff reform rather than a permanent smuggling culture.**  
The tea case makes a useful TCE experiment because the legal price disadvantage could be altered sharply. Run the 1784-style reform through existing merchants and inventories: lawful and unlawful market shares should respond without replacing the population. Mui and Mui’s specialized study is the historical starting point; the tax-rate anchor is independently reported in Antman’s research. [OUP Academic](https://academic.oup.com/ahr/article-abstract/74/1/44/145763)

**Ming China: suppressing commerce can interact with maritime violence.**  
Kung and Ma study intensified trade suppression in **1550–1567**. Their historical dataset finds a disproportionately large increase in pirate attacks in coastal prefectures with greater silk-production potential. The reusable mechanism is frustrated commercial opportunity interacting with coercion—not an ethnic or geographic “piracy propensity.” [Cambridge University Press](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-economic-history/article/autarky-and-the-rise-and-fall-of-piracy-in-ming-china/DFA290E2C6550AF9F29E24039B3314AC)

**Benin, Togo and Nigeria: neighboring policies generate an interconnected trade system.**  
Golub’s fieldwork and customs analysis documents extensive transshipment toward Nigeria in goods it heavily protected, including used cars, cloth and rice. Benin and Togo maintained comparatively low barriers, and Togo used lower transit taxes to offset geographic disadvantages. The lesson is that a neighboring government can benefit from trade that another government considers illicit. [Wiley Online Library](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/abs/10.1111/j.1467-9701.2012.01469.x)

**Japan: unauthorized markets can become everyday urban infrastructure.**  
Hatsuda’s research describes markets occupying accessible cleared or bomb-damaged land, helping reconstruct commerce and shaping later urban space. For TCE, a tolerated unauthorized market can attract stalls, foot traffic, storage and eventually permanent buildings. Illegality should affect exposure and institutional relations, not require invisible transactions. [Sage Journals](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/00961442221078912)

**Sicily: profitable assets and political demand for coercion both matter.**  
Dimico, Isopi and Olsson connect mafia emergence to profitable citrus production under weak protection. Acemoglu, De Feo and De Luca identify a different channel: landowners, managers and politicians supported mafia coercion against peasant organizations. These explanations concern different mechanisms and historical margins; they should not become one deterministic “lemons create mafia” rule. [Cambridge University Press](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-economic-history/article/origins-of-the-sicilian-mafia-the-market-for-lemons/52B18A611BD8AE26B4FDE3814A4239F1)

**Mexico and Brazil: state presence is not a single scale from absent to strong.**  
Mexican enforcement can weaken an incumbent organization while opening space for violent competitors. Brazil’s PCC demonstrates that incarceration itself can coexist with, and contribute to, criminal organizational capacity. TCE therefore needs separate police, judicial, prison, protection and accountability capabilities. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1257/AER.20121637)

The cross-regional implication is to vary **institutions, opportunities and relationships**, not assign regional criminality coefficients.

## 4. Stylized facts and validation targets

These should be **conditional test scenarios**, not outcomes every generated civilization must reach.

| Test scenario | Pattern the simulation should reproduce | Main diagnostic |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Raise a trade duty while holding capacity initially fixed | Greater incentive to conceal trade, possibly with substitution between compliance categories | Physical imports, declared imports and tax receipts must diverge appropriately. Fisman–Wei provides a reporting-response benchmark. [RCNi Company Limited](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/full/10.1086/381476) |
| Prohibit an established consumer good | Initial consumption decline followed by possible partial recovery | A US-like reconstruction permits roughly 30%, then 60–70%, of prior consumption—not automatic zero or full restoration. [National Bureau of Economic Research](https://www.nber.org/papers/w3675) |
| Impose a binding ceiling during scarcity | Queues, ration reliance, alternative procurement and unequal access | Track consumption and full procurement costs, not just official prices; Japan provides a severe mixed-provisioning case. [Dartmouth College Library](https://collections.dartmouth.edu/teitexts/Press_Translations_Japanese/diplomatic/economic-0931-diplomatic.html) |
| Create profitable commerce with weak public protection | Protection specialists may emerge | Their success should depend on appropriable income and political relationships, not simply poverty. [Cambridge University Press](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-economic-history/article/origins-of-the-sicilian-mafia-the-market-for-lemons/52B18A611BD8AE26B4FDE3814A4239F1) |
| Make coordination and monopoly expensive | Persistent small illicit enterprises | The model must allow fragmented markets rather than forcing consolidation. [MIT Press](https://mitpress.mit.edu/9780262181075/disorganized-crime/) |
| Arrest influential leaders without stabilizing territory | Reduced incumbent capacity, potentially increased rivalry | Membership, market concentration and violence can move in different directions. [Sage Journals](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0022002715587053) |
| Improve credible oversight | Less diversion can occur even in a corrupt setting | Olken’s audit experiment supplies a positive enforcement benchmark. [National Bureau of Economic Research](https://www.nber.org/papers/w11753) |
| Establish durable criminal governance | Routine compliance need not require frequent violence | Test reputational sanctions and bureaucratic continuity alongside coercive capacity. [Cambridge University Press](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/american-political-science-review/article/abs/legitimacy-in-criminal-governance-managing-a-drug-empire-from-behind-bars/15AA260804337B58B4A75EAF469457F6) |

Add two **accounting invariants**, which are design requirements rather than historical estimates.

First, under a simplified constant-unit seizure process:

\[
E[\text{seizures}]=\text{illicit flow}\times\text{interception probability}.
\]

More seizures can indicate more enforcement, more trade or both. An official dashboard should not know the true illicit flow unless it has enough evidence.

Second, count value added once. The same grain passing through an unauthorized transporter, wholesaler and retailer does not represent three harvests. Report separately:

**informal labor share; undeclared sales; prohibited transactions; unpaid liabilities; criminal-group income; victim losses; violence; and household welfare.**

A single “crime percentage” cannot validate these systems.

## 5. Modeling recommendation for individual agents and institutions

### 5.1 Minimal reusable entity structure

Use ordinary TCE entities with additional components:

| Entity | Additional state |
| --- | --- |
| Person or household | Needs, liquidity, legal alternatives, perceived sanctions, norm attitudes, relationships and obligations |
| Enterprise | Real inventories, reported records, licenses, workers, counterparties and protection arrangements |
| Transaction or shipment | Quantity, ownership, price, jurisdiction, declaration and observed evidence |
| Organization | Members, treasury, decision rules, reputation, claims, obligations and succession |
| Public institution | Budget, personnel, caseload, information, jurisdiction and accountability |
| Location | Accessibility, storage, commercial activity, inspection exposure and competing protection claims |

Keep **world truth** separate from **institutional knowledge**. The kernel knows the real transaction; a customs officer may know only a declaration, a witness account or an inspection result.

A person should not become globally “criminal.” They may be a farmer, occasional unauthorized seller, victim of extortion and member of a lawful cooperative simultaneously.

### 5.2 Update at different timescales

For the 10,000–50,000-person target, the expensive part should not be repeated global search.

**Transactions and movements:** resolve through existing logistics and encounter systems. Evaluate compliance when goods change hands, enter jurisdictions or are inspected.

**Daily:** update stocks, household procurement, cash, simple beliefs and outstanding obligations.

**Weekly:** reconsider suppliers, employment, occasional illicit opportunities, membership and protection arrangements.

**Monthly or institutionally triggered:** revise enforcement budgets, laws, licenses, organizational strategies and political relationships.

**Event-driven:** resolve confiscation, discovery, arrest, death, leadership succession and collective refusal.

Use bounded candidate sets from existing spatial and social networks. With \(N\) agents and \(k\) considered relationships per decision cycle, candidate evaluation can be approximately \(O(Nk)\), rather than all-pairs \(O(N^2)\). That is an architectural target, not a performance benchmark for the full simulation.

### 5.3 Let organization emerge from repeated cooperation

A proposed lifecycle is:

**occasional cooperation → repeated partnership → shared funds and obligations → durable organization → possible territorial or political authority.**

Each transition should require actual benefits and continued participation. Groups can dissolve, fragment, merge or shift into lawful activity. Arresting a leader should consult succession rules and relationships rather than automatically deleting the organization.

Make coercion compete with alternatives. Households can refuse, report, coordinate, relocate or seek another protector, but each option has costs and constraints. This produces persistent bad equilibria without making agents irrationally passive.

### 5.4 What to simplify first

Start with **a staple, a monopoly good and a discretionary good**. Their different needs, storability and supply responsiveness are sufficient to test ration evasion, tariff smuggling and prohibition.

Reuse ordinary workshops, warehouses, markets, homes and transport assets. Show observable consequences in Unreal: queues, closed stalls, inspections, changed deliveries, confiscated stock, guards, hearings and rebuilding.

Initially omit detailed concealment tactics, elaborate financial laundering and many specialized criminal occupations. The essential dynamics come from **scarcity, accounting, relationships, governance and constrained enforcement**.

Do not simplify away production capacity, household survival, public budgets or information limits. Those are what prevent illicit trade from becoming a costless alternative economy.

### 5.5 Existing models and games to borrow from

| Model or game | Reusable pattern | Limitation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Becker, “Crime and Punishment” (1968)** | Expected benefits, sanctions and costly enforcement | A behavioral baseline, not a complete model of trust, norms or organization. [RCNi Company Limited](https://www.journals.uchicago.edu/doi/abs/10.1086/259394) |
| **GLODERS / Palermo protection-racket model; Székely, Nardin and Andrighetto (2018)** | Entrepreneurs, consumers, mafiosi, police and civic organizations; interaction between reporting, norms and enforcement | Calibration includes Palermo extortion/reporting patterns, but evidence is incomplete and policy outcomes remain simulation results. Borrow the interaction structure, not its population ratios. [Wiley Online Library](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1155/2018/3568085) |
| **Calderoni et al., recruitment ABM (online 2021; volume 2022)** | Household, kinship, work, friendship and co-offending networks; recruitment and population turnover | Palermo/Sicily calibration; 3,000 agents over 30 simulated years. It does not by itself supply a commodity economy or endogenous prohibition system. [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10940-020-09489-z) |
| **BAMERS: Bottom-up Adaptive Macroeconomics with Extortion** | Connecting extortion to macroeconomic outcomes | Useful design/code reference; not a source of universal historical parameters. [CoMSES Net](https://www.comses.net/codebases/e03074bc-714e-47b3-b01d-c0b6b2e61380/releases/1.1.0/) |
| **City of Gangsters** | Illegal production chains, distribution, favors, debt and police relationships | A designed prohibition setting, not evidence that laws or crime organizations emerge historically in the same way. [Steam Store](https://store.steampowered.com/app/1386780/City_of_Gangsters/%3Fl%3Dkoreana%26curator_clanid%3D29006382) |

The GLODERS study is particularly relevant to policy feedback: its simulations suggest that legal intervention and changes in social norms can complement each other, while removing interventions can undo gains or expose people to retaliation. **Treat those as hypotheses to test in TCE, not measured policy effect sizes.** [Wiley Online Library](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1155/2018/3568085)

### Datasets and reusable research materials

**ILOSTAT and the ILO statistical reports** are the appropriate starting point for employment informality. Preserve each series’ definition, coverage and reference period; do not transform employment shares into output shares. [International Labour Organization](https://www.ilo.org/publications/women-and-men-informal-economy-statistical-picture-third-edition)

**World Bank Informal Economy Database:** the consulted release covers up to **196 economies, 1990–2020**, with **11 measures** spanning model-based output, employment and perceptions. Its multiplicity is useful: TCE can be compared against several observables instead of one assumed latent “informality” variable. Cite Elgin, Kose, Ohnsorge and Yu’s *Understanding Informality* when using it. [World Bank](https://www.worldbank.org/en/research/brief/informal-economy-database)

**Medina–Schneider’s country estimates** provide a long-run macro comparison, but should be used as broad scenario bounds rather than precise criminal-sector calibration. [IMF](https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/wp/2018/wp1817.pdf)

**Dell’s article includes replication materials** for the trafficking-network analysis. **GLODERS and the recruitment ABM provide code or supporting materials**, making them useful for examining decision rules, calibration and experimental design rather than only reading conclusions. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1257/AER.20121637)

**Dartmouth’s Japanese press-translation collection** offers unusually concrete contemporary observations, but its household samples and reported prices require source criticism; they are not representative national accounts. [Dartmouth College Library](https://collections.dartmouth.edu/teitexts/Press_Translations_Japanese/diplomatic/economic-0931-diplomatic.html)

### Where confidence should remain limited

**Hidden activity is selected activity.** Police files disproportionately reveal discovered organizations; surveys miss or underreport sensitive behavior. The organized-crime modeling literature explicitly identifies the lack of longitudinal individual-level data as a major constraint. [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10940-020-09489-z)

**Macro correlations are not policy-response coefficients.** A country with high taxes and extensive informality may also differ in enforcement, productive structure and institutional quality. Model-based shadow estimates cannot independently validate every causal assumption used to construct them. [IMF](https://www.imf.org/-/media/files/publications/wp/2018/wp1817.pdf)

**Historical origins are contested and context-specific.** Citrus rents, land conflict and appropriable crops are mechanisms to compare, not historical scripts. The early-state appropriability literature now includes a published critique questioning the breadth and robustness of parts of the evidence. [Cambridge University Press](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/journal-of-economic-history/article/origins-of-the-sicilian-mafia-the-market-for-lemons/52B18A611BD8AE26B4FDE3814A4239F1)

**Policy success has several dimensions.** Lower consumption, fewer illicit transactions, less violence, greater household welfare and weaker criminal political power are not interchangeable outcomes. Prohibition and enforcement research demonstrates why TCE should measure them separately. [National Bureau of Economic Research](https://www.nber.org/papers/w3675)

**The best first implementation is therefore a general institutional model:** real goods and needs, jurisdiction-specific rules, limited information, trusted relationships, optional collective organization and competing providers of protection. Smuggling, black markets and organized crime should be possible equilibria of that system—not mandatory stages or fixed population categories.
