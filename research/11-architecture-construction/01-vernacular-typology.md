## Prompt

```text
Deep research request: A global taxonomy of vernacular architecture

Context: I'm building The Civilization Engine (TCE), an endless, realistic agent-based simulation. Autonomous people found settlements, grow them into cities, and develop their own governments, laws, economies, technologies and architecture from authored building blocks. Nothing about history is scripted, and every world turns out differently. A Rust simulation kernel simulates 10k–50k individual people with visible daily life over decades to centuries, starting from early agrarian technology with no fixed eras. Unreal Engine 5 renders it on a Windows PC. TCE's style primitives (roof forms, wall systems, openings, ornament) come from real vernacular architecture.

Research question: What is the full vocabulary of vernacular building forms and elements worldwide, and what determines which ones a culture uses?

Cover:
- Roof forms and pitches; wall systems (timber frame, log, wattle and daub, rammed earth, adobe, stone, brick); foundations; openings; ornament
- Climate, material and technology determinants (e.g. Oliver's Encyclopedia of Vernacular Architecture of the World)
- Representing styles as combinable parameters
- Regional exemplars with their key parameters

Deliver a simulation-ready report:
1. Mechanisms: the causal drivers and processes, stated as rules a simulation could implement.
2. Parameters: quantitative values and ranges in tables, with units, sources and confidence.
3. Variation: how this differed across eras (foragers, early farming, pre-industrial, industrial, modern) and across world regions, not only Europe.
4. Stylized facts: real-world patterns a correct simulation should reproduce, with numbers where possible.
5. Modeling recommendation: how to represent this with individual agents and institutions, what to simplify, and existing models or games that already do it.
6. Sources: cite scholarly sources and datasets; flag contested claims and thin evidence.
```
