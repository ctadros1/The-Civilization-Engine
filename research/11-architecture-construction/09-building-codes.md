# Building codes and regulation: a simulation-ready report for TCE

## Main finding

**Building regulation was not one invention.** Builder liability, customary obligations to neighbors, restrictions on materials and height, construction inspections, and public-health controls developed through different institutions. Hammurabi’s provisions principally assigned responsibility after defective construction; Rome also imposed preventive restrictions; nineteenth-century legislation increasingly joined technical requirements to plans, inspections, and administrative procedures. These should be separate capabilities in TCE, not a single “building codes” unlock. [Avalon Project](https://avalon.law.yale.edu/ancient/hamcode.asp)

Your requirement that an identifiable person propose a law and an institution adopt it is a strong foundation. **The restriction to proposals following fires or collapses is historically too narrow.** Regulation also emerged from neighboring-property disputes, epidemic fears, state construction management, and the planning of new settlements. China’s *Yingzao Fashi* and the Spanish settlement ordinances of 1573 provide particularly useful alternatives to disaster-driven lawmaking. [Academia](https://www.academia.edu/1304565/Partial_Translation_With_Critical_Introduction_Notes_and_Selected_Commentaries_On_Ibn_al_Ramis_Kitab_al_ILan_bi_Ahkam_al_Bunyan)

The recommended causal chain is:

**Experienced problems → competing explanations → proposed remedies → political authorization → affordable construction choices → imperfect enforcement → changing buildings and risks.**

Neither passage nor compliance guarantees safety: an adopted rule can be technically mistaken, narrowly applicable, inadequately enforced, or undermined by later alterations.

## 1. Mechanisms: rules a simulation can implement

### 1.1 Separate liability from preventive regulation

Hammurabi’s eighteenth-century BCE collection illustrates **outcome-based builder responsibility**. Sections 229–233 prescribe execution of a builder when defective construction kills the owner, retaliatory punishment of the builder’s son when the owner’s son dies, replacement when an enslaved person is killed, compensation and rebuilding for property losses, and repair of unstable walls at the builder’s expense. These are status-dependent remedies, not specifications for foundations, wall thickness, or permissible spans. [Avalon Project](https://avalon.law.yale.edu/ancient/hamcode.asp)

The collection also served royal and scribal purposes. Its relationship to actual legal practice should not be equated automatically with a modern, uniformly enforced statute book; Barmash’s scholarship emphasizes its construction as an expression of justice and royal authority. [OUP Academic](https://academic.oup.com/book/31901)

**TCE rule:** A society may punish a builder without possessing preventive standards, inspectors, or a reliable explanation of the failure. Store separately:

* Who can be held responsible: owner, contractor, master builder, supervising official.
* What must be established: defect, negligence, contractual breach, or simply an adverse outcome.
* Who can bring a claim and whose injuries count under that society’s law.
* What remedy is authorized and whether it can actually be imposed.

Liability should alter perceived incentives and professional reputation—not retroactively increase structural capacity.

### 1.2 Disasters create political opportunities, not automatic reforms

There are direct historical examples of catastrophe followed by targeted intervention. After the amphitheater collapse at Fidenae in 27 CE, Tacitus reports a senatorial requirement for financially qualified show promoters and construction on ground whose solidity had been established. After Rome’s fire of 64 CE, measures addressed street layout, building height, construction materials, water availability, and firefighting equipment. [Penelope](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Tacitus/Annals/4E%2A.html)

London’s rebuilding legislation after the 1666 fire prescribed brick or stone exterior walls, differentiated house classes, and sworn surveyors. Nonconforming construction could be ordered corrected or demolished. The material rules retained explicit allowances for wooden components; they did not create a wholly noncombustible city. [DHI](https://www.dhi.ac.uk/strype/TransformServlet?display=normal&page=book1_233)

**TCE rule:** An incident increases attention according to deaths, displaced households, destroyed wealth, interrupted commerce, and proximity to influential people. It generates several possible responses: punishment, rebuilding assistance, infrastructure investment, ritual action, technical restrictions, or no intervention.

Passage should depend on an existing decision procedure—decree, council agreement, assembly vote, judicial precedent—not an assumed universal majority vote.

### 1.3 Allow non-disaster proposal triggers

The 1103 sanctioning material for China’s *Yingzao Fashi* concerns the revision and circulation of official construction guidance, including material and labor allowances. It represents administrative standardization of state building, not evidence that every private dwelling faced the same requirements. [Project CloudCastle](https://chinesearchitecture.wordpress.com/2017/04/01/yingzao-fashi-0-2-letter-of-sanction/)

The 1573 Spanish ordinances for new settlements prescribed matters including street arrangement, climate-sensitive street widths, hospital placement, and the location of polluting activities. These were prospective settlement instructions, not merely responses to a local fire. [HUD User](https://www.huduser.gov/portal/sites/default/files/pdf/The-Laws-of-the-Indies.pdf)

**TCE rule:** Permit proposals from complaints, procurement audits, settlement founding, institutional borrowing, and observed disease as well as disasters. Formal adoption still requires an agent and an authorized institution.

For a strictly disaster-gated version, allow customary obligations and contractual specifications beforehand, but recognize that this deliberately excludes important historical pathways.

### 1.4 Proposals depend on available knowledge—and can diagnose the wrong cause

The Spanish ordinances distinguished hospitals for contagious disease and referred to harmful winds when prescribing their placement. England’s Public Health Act 1875 empowered local building bylaws covering structural stability and fire prevention alongside ventilation, surrounding air space, drainage, and sanitary facilities. Regulatory purposes therefore overlapped without requiring modern scientific explanations. [HUD User](https://www.huduser.gov/portal/sites/default/files/pdf/The-Laws-of-the-Indies.pdf)

**TCE rule:** An inquiry should produce an *attributed cause*, separate from the simulation’s actual cause. Agents choose remedies from techniques and explanations they know.

For example, a collapse caused by undermined foundations might produce a height restriction rather than a foundation rule. Subsequent experience can strengthen, modify, or discredit that explanation. Evidence from a neighboring city should be available through travelers, builders, officials, and texts—not automatically shared worldwide.

### 1.5 Regulation redistributes costs, so its details are political

Historic requirements were often conditional rather than universal. In nineteenth-century Istanbul, rules distinguished timber from masonry construction and changed over time; some arrangements allowed poorer owners to retain timber construction with fire precautions while requiring masonry from wealthier builders. [Journal Agent](https://www.journalagent.com/megaron/pdfs/MEGARON_11_1_1_14.pdf)

London’s party-wall provisions addressed a coordination problem directly: the first builder could construct the shared wall, with the neighboring owner subsequently reimbursing a prescribed share and interest. Regulation was therefore also a mechanism for assigning costs between owners. [DHI](https://www.dhi.ac.uk/strype/TransformServlet?display=normal&page=book1_233)

**TCE rule:** Evaluate each proposal’s consequences for owners, tenants, trades, creditors, and the treasury. A brick requirement might benefit brickmakers, burden cash-poor owners, and reduce demand for some carpentry while leaving other timber work untouched.

Allow amendments: narrower districts, phased implementation, subsidies, loans, shared-wall reimbursement, or exemptions. A rejected stringent proposal followed by an affordable partial rule is a valuable emergent outcome.

### 1.6 Enforcement is a sequence of costly actions

The 1875 English and Welsh legislation authorized plan submission, inspection, and alteration or removal of nonconforming work. It also specified administrative procedures and protected certain existing buildings from new bylaws. The existence of technical requirements alone was not the entire regulatory system. [Legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/Vict/38-39/55/enacted/data.html)

Actual behavior could remain noncompliant despite rules and heightened attention. An October 1911 report from New York’s Joint Board of Sanitary Control recorded **25 shops with doors locked during working hours**, after the Triangle fire. That observation is evidence of persistent violations, not a citywide prevalence estimate. [Triangle Fire Memorial](https://trianglefire.ilr.cornell.edu/legacy/BdSanitarycontrol1streportOct1911.pdf)

**TCE rule:** Distinguish notification, inspection, detection, an enforceable order, and execution of that order. Inspectors require time, travel, competence, records, and institutional backing.

For an agent estimating the chance of being sanctioned:

\[
p\_{\text{sanction}}
=
p\_{\text{inspection}}
\times
P(\text{detection}\mid\text{inspection})
\times
P(\text{sanction executed}\mid\text{detection}).
\]

These are conditional probabilities, not an assumption of independent events. In the actual simulation, scheduled visits and decisions should generate outcomes; probabilities can represent agents’ incomplete expectations.

Visible excess height should be easier to detect than concealed poor joints. Corrective orders should create work, payment, delay, and possible appeals.

### 1.7 Scope and building age determine who actually changes

Edo’s fire-protection strategy included a distinction between street-front buildings, which were required to be more fire-resistant, and less resistant construction allowed inside blocks. A heterogeneous district could therefore comply with its rules. [J-STAGE](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/journalcpij/47/3/47_721/_article/-char/en)

New York’s 1916 zoning resolution allowed certain existing nonconforming uses to continue, subject to restrictions on alteration and extension. This is an important distinction between an existing legal condition and a prohibited new project. [NYC.gov](https://www.nyc.gov/assets/planning/download/pdf/about/city-planning-history/1916_zoning_resolution.pdf)

**TCE rule:** Every regulation needs an applicability predicate: jurisdiction, district, street class, occupancy, construction type, ownership category, project date, and alteration status.

Give each rule an activation mode such as **new construction only**, **major alteration**, **change of use**, **periodic inspection**, or **immediate correction of dangerous conditions**. Never rebuild the entire city when a law passes.

### 1.8 Building requirements and public services are complements

Rome’s post-fire measures included protecting water access and making firefighting equipment available. Edo combined building rules with open firebreak areas, stations, and organized firefighting behavior. These were packages, not isolated material bans. [Penelope](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Tacitus/Annals/15B%2A.html)

**TCE rule:** Let councils choose among reducing ignition, slowing spread, improving escape, and accelerating response. A household requirement to keep firefighting equipment should create an inventory and maintenance obligation; it should not be a permanent safety bonus after a one-time purchase.

### 2.3 Suggested TCE sensitivity ranges

**The following are design assumptions, not measured historical parameters.** Use them for sensitivity testing until simulated institutional behavior produces satisfactory case comparisons.

| Parameter | Initial exploratory range | Implementation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Personal disaster-memory half-life | **2–20 years** | Vary by direct experience; institutional records can preserve knowledge beyond individual memory |
| Inspection-capacity ratio | **0.1–2.0** available staff-hours / required staff-hours | Derive from staffing, travel, project volume, and inspection stages |
| Defect detection conditional on inspection | **0.1–0.9** probability per relevant inspection | Different distributions for visible geometry, concealed workmanship, and inspector skill |
| Subsidy for incremental compliance cost | **0–75%** | A policy choice constrained by the treasury, not a generic “development level” |
| Compliance grace period | **0–10 years** | Differentiate urgent hazards from expensive stock-wide retrofits |

Confidence in all five numerical ranges as historical estimates is **low/unvalidated**. Their value is that they expose important uncertainties rather than hide them in a global compliance percentage.

These are **alternative institutional arrangements**, not stages every society must traverse.

| Setting | What the evidence supports | Implication for TCE |

Research at Bull Brook identifies **36 artifact concentrations arranged in a circle**, interpreted as a structured Paleoindian aggregation. It does not establish a building-inspection bureaucracy. | **Colonial southern Africa** | Swanson documents how plague emergencies in the Cape Colony helped precipitate forced African residential removals and segregation policies, amid pre-existing political agendas and resistance. **Epidemics deserve their own trigger channel.** Anxiety over cholera helped catalyze Britain’s 1848 public-health legislation, while the Cape example shows how a health emergency could advance exclusionary policy. A simulation that treats every epidemic response as technically correct sanitation reform will miss this variation. [Policy Navigator](https://navigator.health.org.uk/theme/public-health-act-1848-and-general-board-health)

## 4. Stylized facts a correct simulation should reproduce

### Reform should cluster after crises, but not originate only then

Catastrophes should increase the probability that existing demands obtain political support. Cornell’s Triangle history places the fire within an already active reform and labor environment—not at the invention of workplace safety concerns. A city with organized advocates should respond differently from an otherwise similar city lacking them. [Triangle Fire Memorial](https://trianglefire.ilr.cornell.edu/legacy/legislativeReform.html)

### Stronger rules should not immediately produce uniformly compliant cities

The Florida implementation results and the 1911 locked-door observations support a persistent distinction between formal rules and executed behavior. TCE should permit competent construction with dangerous operations, as well as formally approved plans with defective execution. [IDEAS/RePEc](https://ideas.repec.org/a/bla/rmgtin/v20y2017i3p363-390.html)

### New and old building cohorts should remain visibly different

Grandfathering provisions provide a legal reason for stock heterogeneity, independently of enforcement failure. The 1875 Act explicitly limited the application of new bylaws to certain existing buildings. [Legislation.gov.uk](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/Vict/38-39/55/enacted/data.html)

A useful **mathematical test, not an estimated historical turnover rate**, is:

\[
\text{share of original stock remaining after }t
=(1-r)^t.
\]

With no retrofit, a hypothetical annual replacement rate of 1% leaves about **61%** of the original stock after 50 years; 2% leaves about **36%**. Destruction, expansion, and selective rebuilding should modify this accounting rather than disappear into a “code adopted” bonus.

### Regulations should create families of forms, not identical buildings

London’s differentiated storey classes and New York’s street-width/setback rules constrain different aspects of geometry. The latter can produce tall, receding forms rather than simply suppressing height. TCE should show recognizable legal envelopes while retaining variation in use, wealth, construction date, ornament, and builder choices. [DHI](https://www.dhi.ac.uk/strype/TransformServlet?display=normal&page=book1_233)

### Building appearance can change faster than streets and parcels

Hanson’s analysis finds strong continuity in London’s actual street configuration after the Great Fire alongside substantial transformation of its buildings. Wholesale replacement by the famous unexecuted rebuilding schemes would be the wrong validation target. [UCL Discovery](https://discovery.ucl.ac.uk/18699/1/18699.pdf)

### A patchwork of materials may be legal, not evidence of corruption

Edo’s frontage/interior distinction and Istanbul’s differentiated arrangements show why observed combustible buildings do not automatically reveal disobedience. Validation must compare construction against the rule actually applicable to that parcel and owner. [J-STAGE](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/journalcpij/47/3/47_721/_article/-char/en)

### Similar hazards can produce different legal solutions

Rome’s post-64 rebuilding account required independent walls rather than shared party walls; London’s legislation organized shared-wall construction and payment. Do not prescribe one historically inevitable “fire-safe city” morphology. Local property arrangements and construction practices should influence the selected solution. [Penelope](https://penelope.uchicago.edu/Thayer/E/Roman/Texts/Tacitus/Annals/15B%2A.html)

## 5. Recommended representation in TCE

### 5.1 Keep five states separate

The most important implementation distinction is:

**What the law requires ≠ what the plans promise ≠ what was built ≠ how it is maintained ≠ what officials know.**

A compact state design could be:

| Entity | Essential fields |
| --- | --- |
| **Rule version** | Proposer, adopting authority, effective date, jurisdiction, applicability predicate, requirement predicate, exemptions, retrofit trigger, sanctions, funding |
| **Building** | Actual materials and geometry, structural attributes, construction stages, concealed defects, maintenance, alterations, use, occupancy and escape configuration |
| **Owner–builder agreement** | Agreed specification, responsible parties, payment schedule, warranties/liability, certification |
| **Regulatory institution** | Legal powers, budget, staff, competence, travel/service area, backlog, records, appeal route |
| **Inspection or dispute case** | Allegation, observed evidence, uncertainty, relevant rule version, finding, order, deadline, follow-up and outcome |
| **Agent beliefs** | Perceived hazard, attribution of past failures, expected detection, trust in builders and institutions, remembered losses |

These are proposed data structures. Most roles can belong to existing citizens; they do not require a separate fully simulated bureaucracy for every small settlement.

A village might resolve complaints through an existing elder. A large city might employ surveyors. An imperial construction bureau might inspect a palace but have no authority over private houses.

### 5.2 Author a repertoire of rule primitives

For v1, use a small set of composable requirement types rather than free-form generated law:

| Primitive family | Example predicates |
| --- | --- |
| **Material and assembly** | Exterior wall must use an allowed assembly; roof covering must satisfy a local fire-resistance category |
| **Dimensions and load** | Maximum storeys; height by street class; minimum member size for a span; occupation/load restriction |
| **Separation and access** | Building setback; open firebreak; unobstructed lane; required separation between designated hazardous uses |
| **Neighbor relations** | No discharge onto adjacent property; shared-wall consent and payment; maintenance responsibility |
| **Escape and operation** | Specified exits present, connected to a safe route, and unobstructed during occupancy |
| **Sanitation and siting** | Required drainage arrangement; waste-storage location; separation of specified incompatible activities |
| **Process** | Notice, plan review, foundation-stage inspection, completion approval, periodic reinspection |

The grammar should distinguish **a requirement to achieve a property** from **a requirement to use a named technique**. That permits conservative prescriptive rules, alternative accepted assemblies, and later performance-based reasoning without rewriting the institution model.

### 5.3 Make compliance a choice with constraints

For decisions involving financial penalties, a simplified perceived comparison is:

\[
\text{advantage of complying}
=
\text{private risk avoided}
+\text{reputation/contract benefit}
+\text{expected sanction avoided}
-\text{incremental compliance cost}.
\]

Treat these as comparable utility or present-value terms. Do not mechanically convert capital punishment, shame, or family liability into an ordinary fine.

An owner unable to finance the compliant design should choose among delay, downsizing, relocation, seeking assistance, informal construction, or abandonment. Contractors can separately choose hidden workmanship. Occupants can subsequently compromise escape routes or maintenance.

This separation produces failures for understandable reasons rather than from a single “corruption” roll.

### 5.4 Make physical effects arise from actual construction

Legal compliance should alter the relevant inputs to TCE’s existing structural and hazard models.

For fire, a lightweight neighboring-building model could use:

\[
P(i\rightarrow j \text{ during }\Delta t)
=
1-\exp(-\lambda\_{ij}\Delta t),
\]

where the proposed rate \(\lambda\_{ij}\) depends on distance, exposed surfaces, openings, wind, burning intensity, barriers, and response. Its coefficients require separate fire-model calibration.

For collapse, use actual capacity, demand, defects, foundation condition, and maintenance. Keep distinct vulnerabilities for different hazards; multi-hazard exposure work such as GED4ALL provides a useful attribute vocabulary rather than a universal safety score. [Emerald Publishing](https://www.emerald.com/dpm/article/28/6/752/30087/Data-schemas-for-multiple-hazards-exposure-and)

Casualties should follow the people present, warning, escape, rescue, and injury processes—not building value alone.

### 5.5 Use event-driven computation

For 10k–50k agents, my recommended schedule is:

* Evaluate ordinary legal applicability when a parcel, project, use, or rule changes.
* Run inspection queues and construction-stage checks on daily or work-shift schedules.
* Reassess budgets, proposals, and enforcement priorities less frequently.
* Activate faster hazard and evacuation updates only around incidents.

Index rules by jurisdiction and building category. Cache permitted envelopes, but invalidate them after relevant changes. Represent complaints and enforcement as explicit cases, not a daily all-building/all-law comparison.

For reproducibility, keep the Rust kernel authoritative and send Unreal observable outcomes: altered roof assemblies, setbacks, posted notices, suspended construction, demolished extensions, and reconstructed streets.

### 5.6 Borrow components from existing systems—not their assumptions wholesale

| Model or game | Useful precedent | What TCE must add or change |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **UrbanSim parcel models** | Development feasibility under constraints including floor-area ratio, dwelling density, allowed types, height and setbacks | Constraints are supplied to the documented model. Add endogenous proposals, enforcement, concealed quality, and historical institutions. [UrbanSim Cloud](https://cloud.urbansim.com/docs/general/documentation/urbansim%20parcel%20model%20data.html) |
| **Eco** | Player-created governments and programmable laws | Its automatically enforced laws are the wrong default for historical administration. Borrow composable predicates, but replace automatic enforcement with institutions and agent behavior. [Eco](https://play.eco/) |
| **RoboCup Rescue Simulation** | Coupling disaster conditions, civilian agents, and responding organizations | Use response-system ideas; the documented project is not a model of centuries of building regulation and urban growth. [RoboCup Rescue Simulation](https://rescuesim.robocup.org/home/about-robocup-rescue-simulation/) |

A useful **hypothetical acceptance test** is a fire followed by a proposed masonry-frontage rule. Some owners oppose its cost; the council narrows its scope and funds an inspector; compliant frontages appear while grandfathered interiors remain; a later incident exposes a different weakness. All of that should follow from agents and rule scope, without a scripted historical date.

## 6. Sources, datasets, and evidence limits

### Historical sources worth retaining as authored-rule references

The strongest foundations are the **Hammurabi provisions**, **Strabo and Tacitus**, **London’s Act as transcribed in Strype**, **the 1573 settlement ordinances**, **the Public Health Act 1875**, and **New York’s original 1916 resolution**. Together they expose liability, technical prescription, spatial scope, procedure, finance, and exemptions—not merely headline prohibitions. Their citations appear beside the relevant requirements above.

For interpretation, prioritize **Pamela Barmash, *The Laws of Hammurabi* (2020)**; **Julienne Hanson, “Order and structure in urban design” (1989)**; **Morishita, Yamazaki and Ookubo’s Edo study (2012)**; **Gençer and Çokuğraş’s Istanbul study (2016)**; and **Maynard Swanson, “The Sanitation Syndrome” (1977)**. Each is useful for a different causal mechanism rather than as evidence of a universal sequence. [OUP Academic](https://academic.oup.com/book/31901)

### What remains uncertain

**Statute is not practice.** The historical dimensional anchors are substantially firmer than estimates of how often they were obeyed. The existence of a demolition power does not establish how frequently demolition occurred.

**Surviving evidence is selective.** Capitals, major catastrophes, literate administrations, and disputed cases are better represented than ordinary village construction. This report does not support universal annual probabilities for proposal, enactment, bribery, inspection, or violation.

**Archaeological regularity is institutionally ambiguous.** Standardized components can result from molds, craft traditions, exchange networks, patron requirements, or legal compulsion. Do not infer the last merely from the first. The Indus evidence is particularly important here. [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1303.1426)

**Modern effectiveness estimates are not portable constants.** Monetary wind losses, wildfire destruction, and premodern collapse deaths are different outcomes. Use the studies to validate the existence and direction of mechanisms, then calibrate TCE’s physical and institutional components separately.

**Recommended v1 priority:** implement versioned rules, applicability, construction-stage inspections, owner–builder responsibility, operating violations, and grandfathered stock before adding a large legal vocabulary. A small collection of rules operating through real people, budgets, buildings, and disputes will produce more credible regulation than a comprehensive code that instantly enforces itself.
