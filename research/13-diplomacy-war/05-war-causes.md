# Causes of war and peace: a simulation-ready report for TCE

## Executive conclusion

**TCE should model war as a possible outcome of disputes, bargaining, and collective mobilization—not as a fixed propensity attached to an era or society type.** Resource competition explains what people might fight over; it does not, by itself, explain why they reject trade, migration, compensation, tribute, or negotiated division. Bargaining theory identifies several reasons those alternatives fail, especially private information, incentives to misrepresent strength, and inability to make credible future commitments. [Cambridge University Press](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/international-organization/article/abs/rationalist-explanations-for-war/E3B716A4034C11ECF8CE8732BC2F80DD)

The recommended core is:

**A valuable or threatening situation generates a dispute → people organize support → opponents assess their options using imperfect information → demands and concessions are exchanged → coercion, settlement, or fighting follows.**

Two distinctions are essential. First, **small raids and sustained campaigns need different decision processes**, even though historical sources may classify both as warfare. Second, **peace is an institutional achievement**, not simply a low aggression score: it requires ways to manage claims, restrain participants, verify compliance, and resolve subsequent incidents. Ethnographic evidence includes both recurrent raiding and durable peaceful relationships between autonomous societies. [Wiley Online Library](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1155/2014/658597)

## 1. Mechanisms: causal processes TCE can implement

### 1.1 Resource competition creates stakes, but also alternatives

Land, pasture, water, stored food, livestock, trade access, and dependent populations can make coercion profitable. However, distinguish three situations:

| Situation | Proposed TCE mechanism | Why the distinction matters |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Persistent scarcity** | Households compare adaptation, migration, exchange, redistribution, and appropriation. | A starving population may lack the strength to attack. Scarcity should not automatically spawn an army. |
| **Unpredictable shortage** | Recent shocks increase the perceived value of additional reserves, alternative territory, and control over access. | Actors may seek security against future shortages, rather than merely food today. |
| **Appropriable abundance** | Portable or concentrated wealth raises expected returns to raiding and conquest. | A wealthy neighbor can attract predation even when the attacker is not impoverished. |

Ember and Ember’s cross-cultural study found resource unpredictability and mistrust important in explaining warfare, particularly among nonstate societies. This is evidence for a conditional mechanism, not a universal “food shortage increases war by X percent” coefficient. [Sage Journals](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.1177/0022002792036002002)

**Implementation:** generate disputes over identifiable assets and rights. For a watering place, possible outcomes should include exclusive control, seasonal access, fees, shared maintenance, migration, or seizure. Increasing scarcity changes the value of these options; institutions determine which options are available.

Trade deserves similarly specific treatment. Martin, Mayer, and Thoenig found that bilateral trade dependence could discourage conflict, while broader trade diversification could reduce the cost of losing a particular partner. Subsequent research disputes aspects of the latter relationship. Model **lost counterparty-specific surplus and replacement opportunities**, rather than giving all trade a universal peace bonus. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-937X.2008.00492.x)

### 1.2 Raiding economies depend on individual incentives and collective action

Raiding can provide private returns—loot, livestock, status—while imposing retaliation and insecurity on a much larger population. Participants therefore need not share the same interests as their settlement or ruler.

Mathew and Boyd’s Turkana research demonstrates that substantial raiding coalitions can operate without centralized government. Their study also documents sanctions against cowardice and desertion: collective action is organized through social institutions, not simply produced by a leader’s command. [PubMed Central (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3136302/?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

**Recommended individual decision:**

\[
U\_i(\text{join raid})=
E[\text{personal spoils}]
+\text{status and reciprocal benefits}
+\text{avoided sanctions}
-E[\text{injury and death costs}]
-\text{foregone work}.
\]

A raid forms only when enough actual people join and the party can reach its target. Spoils must be divided among participants and patrons. Victims must actually lose assets.

Keep the **raid organizer**, **participants**, and **political authority** distinct. An unauthorized raid can enrich a faction, embarrass a ruler, undermine a treaty, or force an unwanted escalation.

### 1.3 Bargaining failure: why fight when fighting destroys value?

For a deliberately simplified bargaining test, let a divisible stake have value \(V\), let \(p\) be the commonly understood probability that A wins, and let war cost each side \(c\_A\) and \(c\_B\). A peaceful allocation giving A share \(x\) is acceptable to both when:

\[
p-\frac{c\_A}{V}\le x\le p+\frac{c\_B}{V}.
\]

The interval exists because fighting is costly. With different beliefs, both sides can instead believe the other is demanding more than its military prospects justify. Fearon’s important addition is that information is not automatically revealed: actors can benefit from exaggerating strength or resolve. [cambridge.org](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/international-organization/article/abs/rationalist-explanations-for-war/E3B716A4034C11ECF8CE8732BC2F80DD?utm_source=chatgpt.com)

**Implementation:** each political decision-maker maintains its own estimates of enemy strength, willingness to bear losses, reserves, and alliance support. Negotiation uses those estimates, not omniscient kernel values. Reports can be delayed, incomplete, or strategically misleading.

This simplified equation is a useful unit test, not a complete war model: multiple assets, unequal valuations, leader incentives, and future power changes complicate the bargaining space.

### 1.4 Commitment problems: an acceptable agreement today may be unsafe tomorrow

Powell shows how rapid shifts in power can undermine agreements even without simple misunderstandings. A rising power may promise restraint today but have no incentive to honor that promise later. Control of an asset can itself change future bargaining power. [Cambridge University Press](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/international-organization/article/abs/war-as-a-commitment-problem/65DFFF1CD73A16F7ED4EEF6D4F934608)

**TCE rule:** evaluate both immediate settlement value and its effect on future coercive capacity.

Relevant endogenous triggers include a new alliance, a contested succession, a strategically placed fort, acquisition of horses or ships, a demographic recovery, or control of a route needed for future mobilization.

Distinguish:

* **Preventive incentives:** attack before a rival becomes stronger.
* **Preemptive incentives:** attack because striking first is believed to confer an immediate advantage.

Neither should be a generic “rival is growing” modifier. The actor must perceive a specific future threat, lack a credible accommodation, and possess a feasible military option.

### 1.5 Security dilemmas: defensive actions can look offensive

Jervis’s security-dilemma framework explains how actors seeking safety can make others less secure. Cooperation is easier when defense has an advantage and when defensive preparations are distinguishable from offensive ones. [Cambridge University Press](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/world-politics/article/cooperation-under-the-security-dilemma/C8907431CCEFEFE762BFCA32F091C526)

**Implementation:** separate military capability from military posture.

A refuge wall, forward supply depot, concentrated mounted force, and border road should not emit the same diplomatic signal. Their threat depends on location, travel times, likely targets, and what an observer knows.

This permits an endogenous feedback loop:

\[
\text{perceived threat}
\rightarrow \text{mobilization}
\rightarrow \text{neighbor alarm}
\rightarrow \text{countermobilization}.
\]

Diplomatic inspection, advance notification, withdrawal from a frontier, and limits on particular deployments can interrupt that loop. A fort may simultaneously improve defense and worsen relations.

### 1.6 Leaders, factions, and citizens value war differently

Research on leaders and international conflict emphasizes that personal survival and the consequences of losing office affect leaders’ willingness to take risks. A ruler facing violent removal does not necessarily evaluate a gamble like one facing an ordinary electoral defeat. [Cambridge University Press](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/leaders-and-international-conflict/2B2676A409304C47EAA8B4822EFF1217)

**TCE should not optimize a single “national welfare” function.** Instead, use institutionally weighted preferences:

\[
U\_{\text{decision}}(a)=
\sum\_f w\_f\,U\_f(a)
+\text{officeholder-specific benefits and risks},
\]

where \(f\) denotes politically relevant factions and \(w\_f\) follows the actual constitution and power structure.

Landowners may seek territory; merchants may oppose disruption; military followers may demand spoils; households may resist mobilization. Council consent, taxation authority, recruitment rules, and the ability to exit or defect determine whether these preferences matter.

Participatory institutions should not confer universal pacifism. Cross-cultural research finds support for a **dyadic** relationship—participatory polities fighting one another less—not a rule that participatory societies never fight outsiders. [Cambridge University Press](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/world-politics/article/abs/peace-between-participatory-polities-a-crosscultural-test-of-the-democracies-rarely-fight-each-other-hypothesis/2057E8211E9DF5D82E31F976DC77D616)

### 1.7 Revenge and ideology alter what counts as an acceptable settlement

Revenge can connect otherwise separate incidents into a prolonged relationship of hostility. Crucially, attribution matters: retaliation may target an offender, a household, a clan, or an entire polity. Those are different escalation processes. Melanesian evidence explicitly distinguishes attacks on particular people from attacks in which another member of the opposing group can substitute as the victim. [Wiley Online Library](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1155/2014/658597)

**Recommended rule:** represent grievances as records containing victim, alleged offender, attribution confidence, responsible group, demanded remedy, and unresolved balance. Compensation, punishment of an offender, acknowledgment, or mediation can close a grievance without erasing historical memory.

Ideological or sacred claims require more than high monetary valuation. Ginges and colleagues’ experiments found that material inducements could intensify opposition to compromises involving sacred values, whereas symbolic concessions could improve acceptance. This concerns experimentally measured attitudes, not a universal estimate of war onset. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/17460042/)

For TCE, allow some claims to require legitimate procedures, recognition, ritual, or reciprocal acknowledgment. Do not encode a religion or culture as inherently aggressive.

### 1.8 Deterrence and alliances change the options—but are not guarantees

Alliance politics involves both fear of abandonment and fear of entrapment in an ally’s conflict. Different promises create different risks. [Cambridge University Press](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/world-politics/article/abs/security-dilemma-in-alliance-politics/681B1AF11D96E61995028026205CE783)

Represent an alliance as a conditional contract:

| Contract component | Required simulation behavior |
| --- | --- |
| Trigger | Defense against attack, offensive assistance, neutrality, consultation, or a named contingency. |
| Obligation | Troops, transport, supplies, money, access, or diplomatic support. |
| Timing | Mobilization and arrival delays matter; a distant ally may arrive too late. |
| Exceptions | Existing wars, succession, territorial limits, or excluded opponents. |
| Enforcement | Reputation, reciprocal dependence, domestic sanctions, collateral, or coercion. |

An attacker should consider scenarios in which allies join, abstain, or arrive late. Do not simply add all allied population to a strength total. Shared interests also make alliance decisions correlated: several allies may abandon a cause after the same political shock.

**Deterrence succeeds when an attack becomes less attractive than the available alternatives.** A threat need not produce fighting to have an effect. Therefore, calibrating alliance behavior only from wars misses successful deterrence.

### 1.9 Peace-making must solve the next incident, not just end this battle

Fortna’s interstate ceasefire research finds that agreement design can affect peace durability through incentives, information, and management of accidental escalation. Separation of forces, monitoring, guarantees, and dispute-management arrangements are substantive mechanisms. [Cambridge University Press](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/international-organization/article/scraps-of-paper-agreements-and-the-durability-of-peace/70A924CC6B4785C30BADF415244FFA1F)

For TCE, a settlement should specify both **allocation** and **implementation**: who controls an asset, when forces withdraw, who verifies compliance, how compensation is delivered, and who handles violations.

A ceasefire need not resolve the political dispute. It can instead enable negotiation, humanitarian access, or temporary disengagement; success depends on what it was intended to accomplish. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1080%2F13533312.2021.1894934)

War termination should compare the expected future value of continuing with available settlements. Already-incurred losses are sunk costs, although they can create new political consequences or grievances.

Finally, a signatory must be able to bind the relevant participants. A chief’s promise is ineffective when autonomous raiders retain both the incentive and ability to continue fighting.

## 2. Parameters and frequency: what can actually be calibrated?

### 2.1 Define the measurement unit before choosing a number

There is no defensible universal table of “wars per year” for foragers, chiefdoms, and states. The evidence combines ethnographic frequency categories, remembered incidents, archaeological injuries, and threshold-defined modern war datasets. These measure different things. The disagreement is partly substantive and partly about definitions and samples. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23869015/)

TCE should log at least these outputs:

| Measure | Unit and denominator | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Raid incidence | Raids per reachable polity-pair-year | Captures low-cost recurrent coercion. |
| Sustained-war onset | New episodes per at-risk polity-pair-year | Measures escalation without counting every battle as a new war. |
| Polity participation | New war involvements per polity-year | Distinguishes starting a war from joining one. |
| War prevalence | Fraction of polity-days in sustained war | Separates frequency from duration. |
| Violence burden | Deaths per 1,000 person-years, by cause | Separates combat deaths, civilian killing, and indirect mortality. |
| Peace durability | Time from settlement to renewed hostilities | Tests whether agreements work. |

A long conflict with many raids can have high raid incidence but only one sustained-war onset. Conversely, repeated short wars can have numerous onsets but relatively little time under mobilization.

### 2.2 Empirical benchmarks

**Confidence below distinguishes confidence in the reported observation from confidence that it transfers to another society. None of these entries is a universal causal coefficient.**

| Evidence and population | Quantitative observation | Appropriate calibration use | Confidence and limitations |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **Mobile foragers:** Fry & Söderberg, 2013 | **148 documented lethal events in 21 societies**; most were not warfare under the authors’ classification. | Validate that interpersonal killing and organized intergroup warfare remain separate outputs. | Moderate for their coded sample; low for universal prevalence. Observation periods do not supply a common exposure denominator, so this is not an annual rate. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23869015/) |
| **Melanesian societies near European contact:** Younger, 2014 | My tabulation of the paper’s 30-society table: **2 rare, 4 every few years, 6 annual, 18 essentially continuous warfare**. | Supports worlds containing very different local conflict regimes, including recurrent raiding. | Moderate-to-low. These are retrospective ordinal classifications, not complete event histories or a random global sample. [Wiley Online Library](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1155/2014/658597) |
| **Chief-led subset of the same Melanesian sample** | Among **6 societies coded “Chiefs”**: **1 occasional, 3 annual, 2 essentially continuous**. | Demonstrates substantial variation even within one institutional category. | Low generalizability; tiny regional subset. Do not treat five of six as a global chiefdom probability. [Wiley Online Library](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1155/2014/658597) |
| **Jōmon Japan:** corrected Nakao et al., 2016 | **19 injured individuals among 1,051 adults** at sites with at least ten skeletal remains: **1.81%**. | A low skeletal-violence comparator; useful for rejecting uniformly extreme violence across all nonstate settings. | High confidence in corrected counts; low as a war-mortality estimator. Not annual mortality and not all injuries necessarily represent inter-polity war. [PubMed Central (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5134052/) |
| **Turkana raiding:** Mathew & Boyd, 2011 | **88 sampled raids**; stealth parties averaged **12 people**; force raids averaged **315**, median **248**. | Validate different raid-organizing processes and highly variable coalition sizes. | Moderate-to-high for this case; low transfer to small prehistoric settlements. Contemporary pastoralist conditions are not a pristine prehistoric baseline. [PubMed Central (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3136302/) |
| **Interstate war severity:** Clauset, 2018 | **95 COW interstate wars**, with onsets spanning **1823–2003**, form the analyzed sample. Severity is highly uneven and heavy-tailed. | Validate many relatively smaller conflicts and a small number of disproportionately destructive wars. | High for the catalog analyzed; uncertainty about distributional form, temporal change, and transfer to small worlds. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29507877/) |
| **Alliance fulfillment:** Leeds, 2003 | Approximately **75% of wartime alliance commitments were fulfilled**, using treaty-specific obligations in **1816–1944**. | Historical check on triggered treaty compliance. | Moderate; conditional on commitments being tested in war. Not a universal 75% loyalty roll, and not an estimate of deterrence success. [Cambridge University Press](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/international-organization/article/abs/alliance-reliability-in-times-of-war-explaining-state-decisions-to-violate-treaties/EC9C08122EC24E9800F7AFE6000672E4) |
| **Interstate ceasefires:** Fortna, 2003 | In one **47-spell** specification, moderately strong agreements had an estimated renewed-war hazard ratio of about **0.43** relative to weak agreements. | Evidence that settlement design can materially alter recurrence. | Moderate association, low portability as a causal multiplier; small historical sample and observational design. [Columbia University](https://www.columbia.edu/~vpf4/scrapstext.pdf) |

**The practical frequency conclusion:** frequent warfare in ethnographic classifications can mean recurrent raids or seasonal fighting, not an entire population campaigning continuously. Equally, sparse recorded collective violence in a mobile-forager sample does not establish universal prehistoric peace. TCE needs both possibilities without assigning either to an obligatory evolutionary stage. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23869015/)

### 2.3 Dataset thresholds are measurement rules, not behavior parameters

COW’s interstate-war definition uses organized, sustained combat and a threshold of **1,000 battle-related combatant fatalities within twelve months**. UCDP’s state-based armed-conflict threshold is **25 battle-related deaths in a calendar year**; its annual “war” intensity threshold is **1,000**. These definitions are not interchangeable. [Correlates of War](https://correlatesofwar.org/wp-content/uploads/Inter-StateWars_Codebook.pdf)

Do not transplant those absolute thresholds into a world containing 10,000–50,000 people. Instead, retain raw events and expose several analytical classifications: organized raid, sustained war, mobilization burden, and casualty intensity.

Below a dataset’s threshold does not mean peaceful.

### 2.4 Proposed engineering priors—not historical estimates

The following are **starting ranges for sensitivity testing**, with low historical confidence. They are deliberately attached to mechanisms rather than to “aggression by era.”

| Proposed parameter | Initial test range | Interpretation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Routine strategic reconsideration | **7–30 simulated days** | Reconsider immediately after major events; do not wait for the next routine tick. |
| Uncertainty about enemy deployable strength | **0.2–0.6 coefficient of variation** | Reduce with recent reliable observation; increase with stale intelligence and concealment. |
| Ordinary planning horizon | **1–5 years** | Values access, revenues, retaliation, and recovery beyond the immediate campaign. |
| Dynastic or institutional planning horizon | **5–20 years** | Only actors with relevant expectations should value consequences this far ahead. |
| Unreinforced personal grievance half-life | **2–10 years** | A sensitivity parameter, not a claim about human psychology. Institutional transmission can preserve a grievance much longer. |
| Temporary truce duration | **0.25–5 years** | Negotiable term; expiration opens reconsideration rather than automatically causing war. |
| Strategic outcome scenarios per major decision | **8–32** | Sample uncertainty about mobilization, allies, and campaign results. Requires profiling; not a performance guarantee. |

Material war costs should come from TCE’s existing people, equipment, food, transport, and production systems—not from an additional arbitrary “war cost” debit that double-counts those losses.

### 2.5 Calibrate frequency through exposure and escalation

A useful diagnostic decomposition is:

\[
h\_{AB}
=
\lambda\_{\text{crisis},AB}
\Pr(\text{feasible mobilization, authorization, and failed settlement}
\mid \text{crisis}),
\]

where \(h\_{AB}\) is the sustained-war onset rate for an eligible pair. Maintain a separate process for raids.

This is a decomposition of the model’s behavior, not a requirement to roll a random number for war. Crises should emerge from events and conflicting interests.

**Illustrative arithmetic, not an empirical target:** twenty polities with four relevant neighbors each create forty unordered interaction pairs. At an average onset hazard of 0.01 per pair-year, that produces approximately 0.4 pair-level onsets per world-year. Each polity experiences roughly 0.04 new involvements per year—one per twenty-five years—before accounting for coalition wars and periods already at war.

Thus, a plausible-looking global war count can conceal an implausible individual experience. Calibrate both.

## 3. Variation across eras and world regions

The following are **mechanism combinations**, not mandatory historical stages.

| Setting | Evidence and variation | TCE implication |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Mobile foragers** | The Fry–Söderberg sample contains substantial lethal violence unrelated to warfare. In contrast, the Late Pleistocene Jebel Sahaba cemetery in the Nile valley shows repeated episodes of injury among a sample of 61 individuals. Neither supports a universal peaceful or warlike baseline. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/23869015/) | Mobility and access to alternatives can reduce stakes, but valuable locations, hostile relations, and collective organization can still produce violence. |
| **Early farming societies** | Research on Northwestern European early farmers documents violent conflict, including destruction of communities. The comparatively low Jōmon skeletal-injury proportion cautions against treating all settled or nonstate populations alike. [PubMed Central (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC9942812/) | Stored surplus and immovable investments increase what can be seized or defended. The result depends on settlement geography and institutions, not farming alone. |
| **Chiefdoms and decentralized regional systems** | Pacific cases range from occasional to recurrent warfare. Anderson’s reconstruction of Māori warfare identifies prolonged relatively peaceful intervals as well as periods of intensified conflict; the evidence is selective oral and historical reconstruction, not an annual event census. [Wiley Online Library](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1155/2014/658597) | Permit long lulls, coalition realignment, migration, and renewed conflict. Do not impose a “chiefdom war cycle.” |
| **East African pastoral systems** | Turkana raiding combines mobile wealth, collective benefits, personal risk, and informal enforcement of participation. Large coalitions need not require a state bureaucracy. [PubMed Central (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3136302/?utm_source=chatgpt.com) | Livestock mobility, social sanctions, and recruitment networks can matter more than settlement population or ruler rank. |
| **Pre-industrial Mesoamerican states** | Hassig’s analysis emphasizes political and economic purposes, logistical constraints, and coercive power in Aztec warfare. His work on the conquest also emphasizes Indigenous political alignments and coalitions. [University of Oklahoma Press](https://www.oupress.com/9780806127736/aztec-warfare/) | Allow demands for tribute, submission, and realignment—not only annexation. Coalition changes can outweigh a simple technological ranking. |
| **Inner Asian–Chinese frontiers** | Barfield interprets nomadic–sedentary relations as involving substantial interdependence, rather than an invariant opposition between raiders and settled states. [Wiley-VCH](https://www.wiley-vch.de/de/fachgebiete/geistes-und-sozialwissenschaften/the-perilous-frontier-978-1-55786-324-9) | Make trade, payments, raids, protection, and conquest competing strategies. Destroying a productive neighbor need not maximize returns. |
| **Industrial state systems** | Alliance research emphasizes how mobilization and commitments can change crisis dynamics. Modern war catalogs also distinguish interstate, colonial/extrastate, and internal wars; counting only interstate wars omits important violence. [Cambridge University Press](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/world-politics/article/abs/security-dilemma-in-alliance-politics/681B1AF11D96E61995028026205CE783) | Extend military reach and mobilization through actual transport, administration, and production. Do not infer general peace from declining wars within one classification. |
| **Modern deterrence systems** | Nuclear-peace findings are contested. Rauchhaus found support for strategic stability alongside lower-level risk-taking; Bell and Miller challenged the estimated effects using different methods. [Sage Journals](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/abs/10.1177/0022002708330387?utm_source=chatgpt.com) | Model destructive capability, survivability, escalation risk, and command reliability. Do not use a “nuclear states cannot fight” rule. |

Historical contact is a major complication. An ethnographic account may reflect prior displacement, introduced weapons, incorporation into a larger economy, or suppression of warfare. Conversely, a peaceful interval may be mistaken for a permanent condition. These are reasons to preserve uncertainty, not to discard ethnography. [Wiley Online Library](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1155/2014/658597)

## 4. Stylized facts a correct simulation should reproduce

### Heterogeneity and peaceful relationships

The same technological world should support hostile frontiers, occasional disputes, and durable peaceful clusters. Research on peace systems identifies relationships in which neighboring autonomous societies avoid war and sustain positive intergroup connections. This should be an attainable outcome of TCE institutions and relationships—not a special scripted exemption. [Nature](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41599-020-00692-8)

### Different scales of collective violence

Small raiding parties and much larger coalitions should arise from different organizing opportunities. A model that always deploys the same percentage of a polity’s population will miss the contrast illustrated by Turkana stealth and force raids. [PubMed Central (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC3136302/)

### Uneven, occasionally catastrophic war losses

War severity should not cluster tightly around an average. Clauset’s interstate-war analysis supports heavy-tailed variation and also shows why a peaceful-looking interval is insufficient by itself to establish a permanent structural break. Reproduce substantial variation; do not force one universal power-law exponent. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29507877/)

### Preparation without actual fighting

Military investment should sometimes deter attacks. Fortifications therefore cannot be treated as a direct count of wars. Anderson records thousands of Māori pā sites but emphasizes dating and interpretation problems; fewer than 2% had been radiocarbon dated in the inventory discussed. [ANU Press](https://press-files.anu.edu.au/downloads/press/n9214/html/ch02.xhtml)

### Treaties that matter without guaranteeing peace

Stronger settlement arrangements should often improve durability, but succession, changing power, unauthorized attacks, and implementation failure must remain possible. A treaty that only changes an opinion score, or provides inviolable immunity, cannot reproduce the mechanisms examined in ceasefire research. [Cambridge University Press](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/international-organization/article/scraps-of-paper-agreements-and-the-durability-of-peace/70A924CC6B4785C30BADF415244FFA1F)

### Private gains alongside collective losses

Some wars and raids should be attractive to politically influential actors despite harming the broader population. Conversely, mobilization can fail even when collective defense would benefit everyone. These are distinct leader-incentive and collective-action problems, both supported by the literature. [Cambridge University Press](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/leaders-and-international-conflict/2B2676A409304C47EAA8B4822EFF1217)

### No simple monotonic relationship between organization and violence

More centralized organization can suppress local feuding while increasing the capacity for distant campaigns. TCE should test these outputs separately rather than rewarding or penalizing political complexity through one violence variable. Regional comparative evidence does not support treating institutional categories as homogeneous warfare regimes. [Wiley Online Library](https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1155/2014/658597)

## 5. Recommended TCE implementation

### 5.1 Put causation at several levels

| Entity | State worth retaining |
| --- | --- |
| **Person or household** | Assets, dependents, risk exposure, affiliations, obligations, participation preferences, salient grievances. |
| **Faction or recruitment network** | Followers, patrons, mobilization channels, spoils rules, enforcement ability, political objectives. |
| **Polity institution** | Authority to negotiate, declare, tax, recruit, ratify, and enforce agreements. |
| **Polity relationship** | Contact routes, active claims, treaties, attributed incidents, trade exposure, differentiated reputation. |
| **Decision-maker belief** | Estimated opponent capacity, intentions, reserves, reliability, and information age. |
| **Conflict episode** | Participants, objectives, campaign commitments, casualties, outstanding offers, escalation history. |

This division allows an individual crime to remain a crime, become a feud, trigger an unauthorized raid, or escalate into institutional war depending on attribution and political response.

Do not make cultural identity identical to political authority. People sharing language or custom may belong to different polities; one polity may contain several communities; a peace agreement may bind only some actors.

### 5.2 Use an event-driven strategic loop

The proposed loop is:

**Trigger → construct options → estimate consequences → obtain authority and participation → communicate demands → execute → update beliefs and claims.**

Triggers should include an attack, disputed settlement expansion, blocked access, failed payment, succession, alliance change, or credible report of threatening preparation.

Candidate actions should include doing nothing, adaptation, migration, compensation, arbitration, trade concessions, tribute, mobilization, a limited raid, defensive preparation, and sustained war. An actor should not choose between only “war” and “accept humiliation.”

For each action, estimate:

\[
E[U(a)] =
\sum\_s P(s\mid \text{actor's information})\,U(a,s),
\]

where \(s\) includes plausible mobilization, alliance, and campaign outcomes. Outcomes can be approximated strategically, but execution must consume actual resources and involve actual people.

### 5.3 Keep operational truth separate from strategic belief

The kernel knows where soldiers and supplies are. Rulers should not.

Messages require travel. Reports have sources and timestamps. Mobilization may be concealed, exaggerated, or mistaken. Outcomes update beliefs, but one battle should not reveal every aspect of an opponent’s capacity.

Likewise, no strategic decision should create forces merely because a power score says they exist. Recruitment, equipment, supplies, routes, and domestic compliance remain binding constraints.

### 5.4 Treat peace and war as overlapping activities, not exclusive world modes

Use sustained-war states such as:

`Peace → Crisis → Mobilization → War → Ceasefire → Settlement`

But track **raids, border incidents, negotiations, and treaty violations separately**. Negotiation can continue during war. An isolated raid need not reopen a settled interstate war. A ceasefire can coexist with unresolved territorial claims.

Maintain separate records for a war episode, its campaigns, and its participating coalitions. Otherwise, each battle or newly joining ally can accidentally inflate the war-frequency statistic.

### 5.5 Simplify where individual detail adds little

For 10,000–50,000 people, I recommend a **sparse polity interaction graph**, not all-pairs diplomatic evaluation. Relevant connections arise from reachable territory, trade, kinship, claims, alliances, and recent incidents. Include maritime and long-distance raiding connections, not just shared borders.

Keep detailed grievance memories for involved individuals and politically salient incidents. Aggregate older memories into institutional narratives rather than retaining an ever-growing list consulted by every agent.

Strategic decisions can run weekly or monthly between events. Operational movement, consumption, casualties, and enforcement continue at the kernel’s appropriate resolution. Use deterministic random streams and snapshot-based decision evaluation to avoid outcomes depending on iteration order.

These are engineering recommendations, not benchmarked performance claims.

### 5.6 Existing models and games worth borrowing from

| Model or game | Useful contribution | What not to copy uncritically |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Cederman, 2003, “Modeling the Size of Wars”** | Agent-based state formation and interaction can produce emergent distributions of war size. Polities need not remain fixed units. | Matching a severity distribution does not establish that the model has identified the true causes of war. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0003055403000571) |
| **Turchin et al., 2013, “War, space, and the evolution of Old World complex societies”** | Connects geography, military technology, inter-polity competition, cohesion, and political fragmentation. | Its reported explanatory fit concerns historical patterns of large societies, not a validated universal war-onset rule. Replace coarse attack processes with TCE’s bargaining and mobilization mechanisms. [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1073/PNAS.1308825110) |
| **Victoria 3, documented 2022–2023 diplomatic AI designs** | Diplomatic plays, coalition alignment, backing down, and forward-looking peace evaluation provide useful design precedents. | Avoid dependence on arbitrary truce immunity, narrow concession menus, or a single confidence score. These references describe particular published designs, not a verification of every current game mechanic. [Paradox Interactive](https://www.paradoxinteractive.com/games/victoria-3/news/victoria-3-dev-diary-59-ai) |

**Recommended first implementation:** asset-specific claims, imperfect strength estimates, individual recruitment, actual supply costs, compensation, and enforceable ceasefires. Add elaborate ideology, alliance networks, and institutional bargaining once these basic mechanisms work.

The principal theoretical foundations are Fearon’s *Rationalist Explanations for War*, Powell’s *War as a Commitment Problem*, Jervis’s *Cooperation under the Security Dilemma*, and Snyder’s *The Security Dilemma in Alliance Politics*. Together they provide useful mechanisms, not a jointly estimated universal prediction equation. [Cambridge University Press](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/international-organization/article/abs/rationalist-explanations-for-war/E3B716A4034C11ECF8CE8732BC2F80DD)

### Where the evidence is thinnest

**Universal prehistoric frequency is not established.** Skeletal preservation, burial selection, ethnographic observation windows, and inconsistent definitions prevent a clean global annual rate. Archaeological injury proportions cannot be converted into person-year mortality without additional demographic and sampling assumptions. [PubMed Central (PMC)](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5134052/)

**Institutional correlations are not portable causal constants.** Alliance fulfillment, ceasefire durability, participatory governance, and trade relationships depend on selection and context. The appropriate simulation translation is usually a mechanism that can generate the association—not direct multiplication by a published coefficient. [Cambridge University Press](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/international-organization/article/abs/alliance-reliability-in-times-of-war-explaining-state-decisions-to-violate-treaties/EC9C08122EC24E9800F7AFE6000672E4)

**Long-run trends remain contested.** A model can show extended peace despite unchanged underlying processes, while another can show real institutional change. Validate distributions, durations, regional differences, and mechanisms together rather than treating one aggregate trend as decisive. [PubMed](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29507877/)

**Bottom line:** plausible war frequency should emerge from how often consequential disputes arise, who can organize coercion, what participants believe, and whether acceptable agreements can be enforced. Peace should become durable when institutions make those agreements credible—not when the simulation temporarily disables the war button.
